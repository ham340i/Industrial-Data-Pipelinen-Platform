# Accessibility and equitable usability

Design for different technical backgrounds and working conditions without assuming ability, language fluency or visual acuity. These are planned commitments, not verified accessibility claims.

- Provide keyboard access to pipeline creation, configuration and inspection; validate an alternative to pointer-only dragging.
- Use readable labels, sufficient contrast and text/icon status cues that do not rely on color alone.
- Explain errors with the affected block/field and an actionable recovery step.
- Use consistent terminology, examples and progressive detail for both process specialists and technical users.
- Test with varied workflows and accessibility needs through consensual stakeholder feedback; avoid collecting personal demographics unnecessarily.

Add concrete acceptance criteria and manual/automated checks to relevant UI stories. Record limitations and prioritize barriers that prevent task completion.

## Additional constraints to validate

Confirm language/terminology needs, device/input access and workstation resource limits with actual users. No multilingual or mobile support is implemented. Avoid sample data, validation defaults or role assumptions that systematically exclude less experienced or disadvantaged users. Do not infer demographic needs; invite voluntary feedback and link observed barriers to acceptance criteria. No claim of equitable usability can be made until tested.
